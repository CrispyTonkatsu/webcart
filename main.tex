\section{The problem}
Most people when they are young try to balance a stick with their hand,
this can be quite simple (provided your stick is long enough) but it
represents the core of what control theory as a field is trying to do.

This system is fairly complex. This is because it is a Multiple Inputs Multiple
Outputs(MIMO) system as your hand can move in the $xy$-plane and the pole's
orientation can be described with angles $\theta, \phi$.

This problem isn't immediately applicable to real-world problems but serves as
the foundation of warehouse robots carrying boxes (remove the fixed point),
rockets (add thrust), etc.

\section{Degrees of freedom}
As mentioned in the introduction, the cart has the following positional
degrees of freedom:
$$
	\vec{x} =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		x \\ y \\ \theta \\ \phi
	\end{bmatrix}
$$

There's an additional four degrees of freedom that stem from the
time derivatives:
$$
	\vec{x'} =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		x' \\ y' \\ \theta' \\ \phi'
	\end{bmatrix}
$$

\section{Formulas of energy in the system}
The system has potential energy just from the angle $\theta$, as it is
affected by gravity.

$$
	V = m_P gl\cos(\theta)
$$

On the other hand, the potential energy depends on the cart and the pole
$$
	T = T_C + T_P
$$

Representing the tip's coordinates in terms of $\theta, \phi$:
\begin{align*}
	x_p & = x_c + l \sin(\theta) \cos(\phi) \\
	y_p & = y_c + l \sin(\theta) \sin(\phi) \\
	z_p & = l \cos(\theta)                  \\
\end{align*}

After expanding and solving for the tip's kinetic energy in simpler terms:
$$
	T = \frac{1}{2}m_c(x'^2 + y'^2) + \frac{1}{2}m_p v_p^2
$$

Given the terms of $v_p$ are relatively bloated, therefore I'll be using
python and sympy to get a simpler solution.
